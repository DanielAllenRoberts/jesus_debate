# C-A003 — Nicene / classical Christianity is a later political-theological construction

**In short:** Anti argues that Nicene / classical Christian doctrine was substantially built through later councils, politics, and theological development—not simply taught by the historical Jesus. Early Christianity was **pluriform** (many competing versions). This attacks continuity between Jesus and later orthodoxy; it does not by itself deny that Jesus lived. Pro answers with early high Christology and devotion already in the first generation.

## Statement

The doctrinal package of classical (esp. Nicene) Christianity was substantially constructed through later councils, politics, and theological development rather than being a direct, continuous teaching of the historical Jesus.

## Why it matters

Attacks continuity between [[C-003|Jesus Son of God died for sins]] and 1st-century Jesus; does not by itself refute historical Jesus [[C-004|Historical Jesus]].

## Tier proposal

Proposed: **supporting** (Anti may argue Core — Neutral tie-break if disputed).

## Arguments for

- [[A-ANTI-004|Christology developed after Jesus]] — Divine-identity beliefs grew after Jesus’ death, not from his teaching.
- [[A-ANTI-005|Orthodoxy as later winner]] — Orthodoxy won among many early Christianities; it isn’t a pure transcript of Jesus.


## Arguments against

- [[A-PRO-005|Resurrection vindicates divine identity and atonement]] — If God raised Jesus, that backs divine identity and “died for sins.” *(via opposing [[C-003|Jesus Son of God died for sins]])*
- [[A-PRO-008|Early creed + high Christology at origins]] — Very early worship/creeds already treat Jesus as exalted Lord. *(via opposing [[C-003|Jesus Son of God died for sins]])*
- [[A-PRO-013|Cultic devotion shows early divine honor]] — Earliest Christians gave Jesus worship-like honor reserved for God.
- [[A-PRO-014|Bauckham divine identity Christology]] — Early texts include Jesus in Israel’s unique divine identity.
