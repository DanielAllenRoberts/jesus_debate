# C-003 — Jesus is the Son of God who died for sins

**In short:** This is the heart of Christian identity claims: Jesus is not only a teacher or prophet but the divine Son, and his death deals with human sin (“atonement” = making right with God). Pro ties this to early worship, creeds, and resurrection vindication; Anti says divine-identity beliefs and Nicene orthodoxy developed later. Terms: **Christology** = beliefs about who Jesus is; **Nicene** = the fourth-century creedal settlement on full divinity.

## Statement

Jesus of Nazareth is the divine Son of God, and his death is salvific atonement for sin.

## Why it matters

Foundation layer 3 of [[Christianity Truth Stack]].

Proposed: **foundation**.

## Arguments for

- [[A-PRO-005|Resurrection vindicates divine identity and atonement]] — If God raised Jesus, that backs divine identity and “died for sins.”
- [[A-PRO-008|Early creed + high Christology at origins]] — Very early worship/creeds already treat Jesus as exalted Lord.
- [[A-PRO-013|Cultic devotion shows early divine honor]] — Earliest Christians gave Jesus worship-like honor reserved for God.
- [[A-PRO-014|Bauckham divine identity Christology]] — Early texts include Jesus in Israel’s unique divine identity.


## Arguments against

- [[A-ANTI-004|Christology developed after Jesus]] — Divine-identity beliefs grew after Jesus’ death, not from his teaching. *(via opposing [[C-A003|Nicene Christianity later construction]])*
- [[A-ANTI-005|Orthodoxy as later winner]] — Orthodoxy won among many early Christianities; it isn’t a pure transcript of Jesus. *(via opposing [[C-A003|Nicene Christianity later construction]])*
