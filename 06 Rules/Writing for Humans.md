# Writing for Humans

> Learner style guide. Bound by [[Constitution]] §12. Scoring math is unchanged — this governs how agents write.

## Audience

Write for an **intelligent non-specialist** who wants to **learn** the dispute, not memorize vault codes. Assume they are sharp and curious; do not assume they already know what `C-006` or `SC-001` means.

## One-document readable

Wire briefings, Breaking notes, Scoreboard narrative, and round/agent logs should make sense **on their own**. A reader should grasp the fight without opening every linked note just to decode an ID.

## Story first; codes secondary

Lead with the story and the idea. Prefer:

- “the claim that Jesus rose from the dead”  
over  
- “C-006”

Codes belong in parentheses or Obsidian display text: `[[C-006|Resurrection]]` or `…Resurrection (C-006)…`. Stable IDs keep the graph; prose carries meaning.

## Every coded mention needs three things

When Wire / Scoreboard / round logs mention a claim, argument, rebuttal, source challenge, or score event:

1. **Human label** — plain language in the sentence or `[[Note|human label]]`
2. **One-sentence explanation** — what the point is
3. **Why it matters** — for the [[Christianity Truth Stack]] and/or the probability story

Violation examples: bare `[[R-001]]`, link dumps (`Rebuttals [[R-001]] [[R-002]]`), or labels with no explanation of the substance.

## Jargon

Define specialized terms **once** on first use in that document (or link [[Glossary]]). After that, use the plain term freely.

## Templates

New notes follow templates in `08 Templates/`. Each includes an **Explain for the reader** block — fill it; do not leave it as a code-only stub.
