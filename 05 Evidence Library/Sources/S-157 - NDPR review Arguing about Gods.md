# S-157 — NDPR review: Arguing about Gods

## Explain for the reader

Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews’ long critical notice of Graham Oppy’s *Arguing about Gods*. Agents use it to pin Oppy’s **success criterion** (arguments that would persuade any reasonable person) and to record that even a theist-friendly reviewer grants the book’s scope while disputing whether that bar settles theism vs atheism.

## Citation

- Title: Arguing about Gods (review)
- Author: Alexander R. Pruss, Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews
- Year: 2007
- URL: [NDPR — Arguing about Gods](https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/arguing-about-gods/)

## Summary (neutral)

Pruss summarizes Oppy’s central claim: there are no **successful** arguments for or against an “orthodoxly conceived monotheistic god,” where success is framed as persuading any (or a non-zero share of) reasonable persons who do not already accept the premises. Oppy proceeds case-by-case through ontological, cosmological, teleological, and “minor” evidential arguments, plus evil. Pruss objects that Oppy has not shown premises can be rejected without doxastic perversity, that cumulative-case dismissal is too quick, and that some historical/miracle discussions are thin — while still treating the book as the most comprehensive critical examination available.
