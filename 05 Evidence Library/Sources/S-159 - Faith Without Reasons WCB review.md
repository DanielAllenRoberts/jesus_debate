# S-159 — Faith Without Reasons? (WCB review)

## Explain for the reader

A substantial review essay of Alvin Plantinga’s *Warranted Christian Belief* that explains the **de jure vs de facto** distinction in plain terms. Pro uses it to show why “theism is irrational” slogans fail; Neutrals use it to warn that WCB (and popular summaries of it) do **not** settle whether Christianity’s full truth-stack is true.

## Summary (neutral)

Anderson reconstructs Plantinga’s project: answering the *de jure* charge that Christian belief is unjustified/irrational/unwarranted **regardless** of truth, as distinct from the *de facto* charge that it is false. Core moves include rejecting classical foundationalist evidentialism, analyzing warrant via proper function, proposing an Aquinas/Calvin *sensus divinitatis* model (and an extended model with internal instigation of the Holy Spirit for full Christian belief), and arguing that many Freudian/Marxian “irrationality” stories smuggle in the assumption that theism is false. Anderson’s assessment: WCB succeeds in showing that **if** Christianity is true, Christian belief can be warranted — and in forcing critics back to the de facto question — while Plantinga does **not** purport to prove the antecedent.
