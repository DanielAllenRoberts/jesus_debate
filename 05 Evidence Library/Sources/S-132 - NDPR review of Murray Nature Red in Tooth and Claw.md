# S-132 — NDPR review of Murray *Nature Red in Tooth and Claw*

## Explain for the reader

Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews critique of Murray’s animal-suffering theodicy project. Argues Murray’s “successful” Causa Dei explanations fail his own plausibility bar and that the Darwinian problem remains a serious threat to theistic rationality. Anti uses it to limit Pro’s evolutionary-theodicy optimism; also documents Darwin’s own worry about deep-time animal pain (as quoted via Murray).

## Summary (neutral)

Summarizes Murray’s Darwinian problem (evolutionary machinery producing vast animal suffering across deep time) and his CD-explanation strategy. Contends neo-Cartesian, Fall/Satan, animal-benefit, and chaos-to-order nomic-regularity CDs fail given justified scientific and philosophical acceptances. Concludes the book is useful pedagogically but does not undermine the evidential problem from animal suffering on Murray’s own terms. Quotes Darwin’s concern that sufferings of “millions of lower animals throughout almost endless time” conflict with unbounded divine goodness (via Murray p. 2).
