# S-139 — Habermas/Bergeron clinical review of psychiatric Easter hypotheses

## Explain for the reader

Pro-side clinical pushback on vision-only / psychiatric naturalisms: an *Irish Theological Quarterly* article (hosted on Habermas’s site) argues that hallucination, conversion-disorder, and bereavement-vision hypotheses for the disciples’ encounters are medically poorly grounded and fail to fit multi-claimant Easter data. Deepens vault Habermas lines ([[S-135|S-135]], [[S-037|S-037]]) with a distinct URL focused on psychiatric alternatives.

## Summary (neutral)

Grants that disciples had experiences they took as encounters with the risen Jesus, then clinically appraises psychiatric alternatives (hallucinations, conversion disorder, bereavement-related visions). Argues these hypotheses are largely absent from peer-reviewed medical literature as Easter explanations and fit poorly with group/varied appearance claims. Apologetic venue/authors — Neutrals should not treat the piece as medical-consensus closure or Proven on [[C-006|Resurrection]].
