# S-137 — Kamp SED bereavement experiences (Schizophrenia Bulletin)

## Explain for the reader

Open-access interdisciplinary review of sensory and quasi-sensory experiences of the deceased (SED) in bereavement — the modern psychology literature on grief apparitions / “sense of presence.” Anti uses it to deepen naturalistic Easter appearance models beyond R06 Lüdemann alone; Pro grants the category while denying automatic identification with first-century resurrection proclamation.

## Citation

- Title: Sensory and Quasi-Sensory Experiences of the Deceased in Bereavement: An Interdisciplinary and Integrative Review
- Author: Karina Stengaard Kamp, Edith Maria Steffen, Ben Alderson-Day, et al. (ICHR working group)
- Year: 2020
- Journal: *Schizophrenia Bulletin* 46(6): 1367–1381
- DOI: [10.1093/schbul/sbaa113](https://doi.org/10.1093/schbul/sbaa113)
- URL: [PMC open access](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7707065/)

## Summary (neutral)

Surveys psychiatry, psychology, and anthropology on bereavement SED (seeing, hearing, touching, or sensing the presence of the dead). Concludes most such experiences are benign and should be read in biographical, relational, and cultural context — not automatically as psychiatric pathology. Does not itself adjudicate the historicity of Jesus’ resurrection; it documents that apparitional bereavement reports are common cross-culturally.
