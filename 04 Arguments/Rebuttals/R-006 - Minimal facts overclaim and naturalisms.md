# R-006 — Minimal facts overclaim consensus and skip viable naturalisms

**In short:** Rebuts [[A-PRO-004|Minimal facts resurrection case]] on [[C-006|Resurrection]]. Habermas-style “minimal facts” packaging overclaims consensus and underplays viable naturalistic explanations. Related: [[SC-001|Challenge to S-047 minimal facts consensus rhetoric]].

- Argument: [[A-PRO-004|Minimal facts resurrection case]]
- Claim: [[C-006|Resurrection]]
- Sources: [[S-047|Habermas, Minimal Facts approach]], [[S-004|Habermas & Licona, Case for the Resurrection]], [[S-005|Licona, Resurrection of Jesus (historiography)]]
- Related challenge: [[SC-001|Challenge to S-047 minimal facts consensus rhetoric]]


## Attack

Type: `credibility` (+ `evidence`)

[[A-PRO-004|Minimal facts resurrection case]] hinges on a “widely granted” data set best explained by resurrection ([[S-047|Habermas, Minimal Facts approach]], [[S-004|Habermas & Licona, Case for the Resurrection]]). Anti challenges both halves. First, “consensus” rhetoric in apologetic minimal-facts literature often packages disputed items (especially empty tomb) as if equal to crucifixion; see also [[SC-001|Challenge to S-047 minimal facts consensus rhetoric]]. Second, even granting death and appearance *beliefs*, Lüdemann-style vision/grief models ([[S-027|Lüdemann, Resurrection of Jesus]], [[S-077|Lüdemann, Resurrection as mourning]]) and Humean priors ([[S-023|Hume, Of Miracles]]) remain live options. Licona’s historiographical ranking ([[S-005|Licona, Resurrection of Jesus (historiography)]]) is insider adjudication, not a neutral court verdict.

Craig–Ehrman debate materials ([[S-051|Craig–Ehrman resurrection debate]]) show a leading critical scholar contesting whether historical method can deliver miracle conclusions. Anti’s effect: [[C-006|Resurrection]] stays Contested; “best explanation” is not Proven.
