# R-001 — Creed timing undercuts vision-only Easter

## Targets

- Argument: [[A-ANTI-001]]
- Claim: [[C-A001]] (also pressures [[C-006]])
- Related Pro case: [[A-PRO-004]], [[A-PRO-011]]

## Attack

Type: `evidence`

[[A-ANTI-001]] explains Easter via grief visions and treats empty tomb as secondary legend ([[S-027]], [[S-077]]). That reconstruction underweights the chronological pressure of the pre-Pauline tradition summarized in [[S-037]] and used in [[S-004]] / [[S-003]]: death, burial, resurrection, and appearances are already packaged in a received creed within years, not decades after 70 CE. Vision experiences may be part of the story; they do not by themselves explain why *burial* is early and why Jerusalem proclamation would invite easy falsification if a body remained available. Crossan’s burial skepticism ([[S-078]]) must overcome that creed clause, not merely Gospel narrative lateness.

Hume’s miracle bar ([[S-023]]) is an epistemic prior, not a historical explanation of the data set. Priors can be high against miracles without supplying the actual causal history of the creed and appearances.
