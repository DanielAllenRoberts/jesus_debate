# R-001 — Creed timing undercuts vision-only Easter

**In short:** Rebuts [[A-ANTI-001|Vision-origin naturalistic Easter]] on claim [[C-A001|Resurrection did not occur]] (and pressures [[C-006|Resurrection]]). Grief visions alone underweight the early pre-Pauline creed that already packages death, burial, resurrection, and appearances within years—not only post-70 legend. Related Pro case: [[A-PRO-004|Minimal facts resurrection case]], [[A-PRO-011|Empty tomb + early creed as resurrection hinges]].

## Targets

- Argument: [[A-ANTI-001|Vision-origin naturalistic Easter]]
- Claim: [[C-A001|Resurrection did not occur]] (also pressures [[C-006|Resurrection]])
- Related Pro case: [[A-PRO-004|Minimal facts resurrection case]], [[A-PRO-011|Empty tomb + early creed as resurrection hinges]]


## Attack

Type: `evidence`

[[A-ANTI-001|Vision-origin naturalistic Easter]] explains Easter via grief visions and treats empty tomb as secondary legend ([[S-027|Lüdemann, Resurrection of Jesus]], [[S-077|Lüdemann, Resurrection as mourning]]). That reconstruction underweights the chronological pressure of the pre-Pauline tradition summarized in [[S-037|Habermas, Experiences of the Risen Jesus]] and used in [[S-004|Habermas & Licona, Case for the Resurrection]] / [[S-003|Wright, Resurrection of the Son of God]]: death, burial, resurrection, and appearances are already packaged in a received creed within years, not decades after 70 CE. Vision experiences may be part of the story; they do not by themselves explain why *burial* is early and why Jerusalem proclamation would invite easy falsification if a body remained available. Crossan’s burial skepticism ([[S-078|Crossan, Who Killed Jesus?]]) must overcome that creed clause, not merely Gospel narrative lateness.

Hume’s miracle bar ([[S-023|Hume, Of Miracles]]) is an epistemic prior, not a historical explanation of the data set. Priors can be high against miracles without supplying the actual causal history of the creed and appearances.
