# R-002 — Post-70 dating does not erase pre-70 creed

**In short:** Rebuts [[A-ANTI-006|Gospel distance allows legendary embossing]] on [[C-006|Resurrection]]. Dating finished Gospels after 70 CE does not erase earlier Pauline/pre-Pauline Easter tradition. Late narrative books ≠ late origin of the core proclamation.

## Targets

- Argument: [[A-ANTI-006|Gospel distance allows legendary embossing]]
- Claim: [[C-006|Resurrection]]
- Source engaged: [[S-076|Why Date the Gospels after 70 CE?]]


## Attack

Type: `logic` (+ `evidence`)

[[A-ANTI-006|Gospel distance allows legendary embossing]] correctly notes that finished Gospel narratives are widely dated after 70 ([[S-076|Why Date the Gospels after 70 CE?]]) and contain theological embossing ([[S-066|Ehrman, Jesus Interrupted]]). Pro grants narrative development. The illicit step is treating Gospel distance as if it equally distances *all* resurrection-relevant tradition. Paul’s letters and the 1 Cor 15 creed materials ([[S-037|Habermas, Experiences of the Risen Jesus]], [[S-004|Habermas & Licona, Case for the Resurrection]]) predate the finished Gospels. Literary dependence among Synoptics ([[S-067|Did Gospel authors know each other?]]) weakens “four independent cameras,” not the existence of early proclamation.

Therefore legendary-embossing arguments police Gospel *detail* more than they refute early Easter *kerygma*. Empty-tomb narrative particulars may be embossed; the early claim that Jesus was raised remains to be explained ([[A-PRO-004|Minimal facts resurrection case]]).
