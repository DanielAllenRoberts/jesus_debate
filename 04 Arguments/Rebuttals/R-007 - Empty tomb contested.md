# R-007 — Empty tomb is contested and narratively embossable

**In short:** Rebuts [[A-PRO-011|Empty tomb + early creed as resurrection hinges]] on [[C-006|Resurrection]]. The empty tomb is historically contested and the narratives are embossable; it should not be treated as a settled hinge that forces bodily resurrection.

## Targets

- Argument: [[A-PRO-011|Empty tomb + early creed as resurrection hinges]]
- Claim: [[C-006|Resurrection]]
- Sources: [[S-048|Craig, Historicity of the Empty Tomb]], [[S-078|Crossan, Who Killed Jesus?]], [[S-076|Why Date the Gospels after 70 CE?]]


## Attack

Type: `evidence`

[[A-PRO-011|Empty tomb + early creed as resurrection hinges]] pairs early creed with empty-tomb historicity ([[S-048|Craig, Historicity of the Empty Tomb]], [[S-003|Wright, Resurrection of the Son of God]]). Anti grants early proclamation more readily than tomb historicity. Crossan ([[S-078|Crossan, Who Killed Jesus?]]) and post-70 Gospel dating ([[S-076|Why Date the Gospels after 70 CE?]]) supply pathways for burial/tomb embossing after visionary faith already existed ([[A-ANTI-001|Vision-origin naturalistic Easter]], [[S-027|Lüdemann, Resurrection of Jesus]]). Craig’s empty-tomb article ([[S-048|Craig, Historicity of the Empty Tomb]]) is advocacy historiography inside a contested specialty, not a settled fact on par with crucifixion.

Even if “buried” appears in early tradition, burial ≠ verified empty tomb discovered by named women as narrated. Anti seeks to sever Pro’s second hinge.
