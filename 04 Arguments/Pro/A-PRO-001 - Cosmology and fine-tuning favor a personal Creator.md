# A-PRO-001 — Cosmology and fine-tuning favor a personal Creator

## Claim targeted

[[C-001]]

Opposing Anti claim: [[C-A002]]

## Thesis

Contingency and kalām-style cosmological reasoning, together with cosmic fine-tuning arguments, make a personal Creator a better explanation of the universe’s existence and life-permitting structure than a complete naturalist package that leaves those features brute or unexplained.

## Argument

The first strand is cosmological. The Stanford Encyclopedia entry on the cosmological argument ([[S-013]]) surveys contingency and causal formulations: why there is a contingent cosmos at all, and whether an infinite regress of contingent explainers is satisfactory. Thomas Aquinas’s “Five Ways” materials ([[S-030]]) and William Lane Craig’s kalām presentation ([[S-031]], [[S-002]]) press versions of “whatever begins to exist has a cause” plus “the universe began to exist.” Pro’s claim here is philosophical, not a laboratory proof: if the universe is contingent or began, theism offers a personal necessary cause where naturalism often stops at a brute fact or an unexplained initial condition.

The second strand is teleological / fine-tuning. SEP’s teleological survey ([[S-014]]) and fine-tuning discussion ([[S-033]]) document the claim that life-permitting physics depends on narrow ranges of constants and boundary conditions. Richard Swinburne’s cumulative case ([[S-012]]) treats fine-tuning as one datum among others raising the posterior probability of theism. Craig’s popular and academic presentations ([[S-002]], [[S-055]], debate [[S-015]]) argue that chance, necessity, and multiverse hypotheses either under-explain or themselves require further explanation.

These strands are cumulative, not individually decisive. A successful objection to kalām (for example, denying a beginning, or denying causal principles outside spacetime) does not automatically defeat fine-tuning, and vice versa. Pro also does not leap from “a Creator” to “the biblical God”; that identification is [[C-002]]’s job via other arguments such as [[A-PRO-007]] and [[A-PRO-002]]. The present argument only supports [[C-001]] against [[C-A002]]’s denial of classical theism.

Anti’s counterweight is primarily the problem of evil and divine hiddenness ([[A-ANTI-002]], [[S-060]], [[S-061]], [[S-062]], [[S-063]]). Under symmetry, those are real Bayesian costs for theism. Pro’s reply at this stage is not to pretend evil is unreal, but to deny that evil/hiddenness alone cancel cosmological and fine-tuning evidence without a full comparative accounting — a fight continued in Phase 3 rebuttals.
