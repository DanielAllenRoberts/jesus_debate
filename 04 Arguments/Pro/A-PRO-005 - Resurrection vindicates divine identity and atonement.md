# A-PRO-005 — Resurrection vindicates divine identity and atonement

**In short:** This argument supports [[C-003|Jesus Son of God died for sins]]. If God raised Jesus, that divine act **vindicates** (publicly backs) Jesus’ identity and the claim that his death was for sins. It depends on the resurrection claim; it is not a free-standing historical proof of divinity.

## Claim targeted

[[C-003|Jesus Son of God died for sins]] — Jesus is the divine Son of God, and his death saves people from sin.

Opposing Anti claim: [[C-A003|Nicene Christianity later construction]] — Nicene-style orthodox Christianity was largely built later, not simply taught by the historical Jesus.


## Thesis

If God raised Jesus, that vindicates divine identity and the claim that his death was for sins.

## Argument

## Argument

If [[C-006|Resurrection]] holds — Jesus bodily raised — then, within Second Temple categories mapped by Wright ([[S-003|Wright, Resurrection of the Son of God]], [[S-050|Wright, Surprised by Hope]]), God has publicly vindicated Jesus. Pro infers that early claims tying Jesus’ death to dealing with sin ([[S-037|Habermas, Experiences of the Risen Jesus]], [[A-PRO-008|Early creed + high Christology at origins]]) and exalted identity ([[S-039|Hurtado, Early High Christology / Lord Jesus Christ]]) gain historical-theological confirmation rather than remaining free-floating dogma.

This argument is explicitly conditional on the resurrection hinge ([[A-PRO-004|Minimal facts resurrection case]], [[A-PRO-011|Empty tomb + early creed as resurrection hinges]]). It cannot strengthen [[C-003|Jesus Son of God died for sins]] beyond whatever status [[C-006|Resurrection]] has. Anti’s developmental Christology ([[A-ANTI-004|Christology developed after Jesus]], [[S-025|Ehrman, How Jesus Became God]]) and pluriformity ([[A-ANTI-005|Orthodoxy as later winner]]) still matter for *how much* identity is vindicated (exalted Messiah vs full Nicene package). Pro claims at least this: resurrection undercuts “mere later invention with no divine endorsement” readings of [[C-A003|Nicene Christianity later construction]].
