# A-PRO-014 — Bauckham divine identity Christology

**In short:** This argument supports [[C-003|Jesus Son of God died for sins]] via Richard Bauckham’s “divine identity” reading: earliest texts include Jesus in the unique identity of Israel’s God (creator/ruler). That is a first-century monotheistic grammar—not automatically every later Nicene technical term, but far earlier than “fourth-century invention” slogans.

## Claim targeted

[[C-003|Jesus Son of God died for sins]] — Jesus is the divine Son of God, and his death saves people from sin.


## Thesis

Earliest Christian texts include Jesus within the unique divine identity of the God of Israel (creator/ruler), so Pro’s Son/identity claim has a first-century monotheistic grammar — not merely a fourth-century metaphysical invention.

## Argument

Richard Bauckham’s *Jesus and the God of Israel* ([[S-103|Bauckham, Jesus and the God of Israel]], popular pathway [[S-104|CT popularization of Bauckham]]) argues that Second Temple monotheism marked out the one God as sole creator and sovereign; NT writers place Jesus inside that identity (e.g., sovereignty, creation association, Kyrios patterns) rather than as a semi-divine intermediary. Combined with Hurtado’s cultic devotion evidence ([[S-096|Hurtado, Early High Christology clarifying]], [[S-099|Hurtado, Devotion to Jesus]], [[A-PRO-013|Cultic devotion shows early divine honor]]) and early creed ([[S-037|Habermas, Experiences of the Risen Jesus]], [[A-PRO-008|Early creed + high Christology at origins]]), Pro claims continuity of exalted identity sufficient to **Strengthen** [[C-003|Jesus Son of God died for sins]] even while granting later Nicene vocabulary ([[S-089|First Council of Nicaea]], [[S-090|First Council of Constantinople / Creed]]) and Anti’s developmental cautions ([[S-025|Ehrman, How Jesus Became God]], [[R-016|Early devotion still post-Easter vs historical Jesus]]).

This still depends partly on resurrection vindication ([[A-PRO-005|Resurrection vindicates divine identity and atonement]], [[C-006|Resurrection]] Strengthened) for full stack force, but identity-inclusion is historically early on Bauckham’s reading.
