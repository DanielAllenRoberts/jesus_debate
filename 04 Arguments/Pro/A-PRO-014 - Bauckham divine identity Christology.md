# A-PRO-014 — Bauckham divine identity Christology

## Claim targeted

[[C-003]]

Opposing: [[C-A003]]

## Thesis

Earliest Christian texts include Jesus within the unique divine identity of the God of Israel (creator/ruler), so Pro’s Son/identity claim has a first-century monotheistic grammar — not merely a fourth-century metaphysical invention.

## Argument

Richard Bauckham’s *Jesus and the God of Israel* ([[S-103]], popular pathway [[S-104]]) argues that Second Temple monotheism marked out the one God as sole creator and sovereign; NT writers place Jesus inside that identity (e.g., sovereignty, creation association, Kyrios patterns) rather than as a semi-divine intermediary. Combined with Hurtado’s cultic devotion evidence ([[S-096]], [[S-099]], [[A-PRO-013]]) and early creed ([[S-037]], [[A-PRO-008]]), Pro claims continuity of exalted identity sufficient to **Strengthen** [[C-003]] even while granting later Nicene vocabulary ([[S-089]], [[S-090]]) and Anti’s developmental cautions ([[S-025]], [[R-016]]).

This still depends partly on resurrection vindication ([[A-PRO-005]], [[C-006]] Strengthened) for full stack force, but identity-inclusion is historically early on Bauckham’s reading.
