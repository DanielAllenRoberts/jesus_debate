# A-PRO-002 — Moral law points to biblical theism

**In short:** This argument supports [[C-001|God exists]] (and pressures the jump toward biblical theism). It says our sense of real moral obligation—“I *ought* not do this”—is better explained if a moral Lawgiver exists than if morality is only preference or evolutionary byproduct. Key term: **objective morality** = right/wrong independent of opinion.

## Claim targeted

[[C-001|God exists]] — A real, personal creator God exists (classical theism).

Opposing Anti claim: [[C-A002|No classical theistic God]] — There is no classical personal creator God of the kind Pro needs.


## Thesis

Objective moral experience is better explained by classical/biblical theism than naturalism.

## Argument

## Argument

C. S. Lewis’s moral argument in [[S-001|Lewis, Mere Christianity]] treats the felt obligation of a moral law as data needing explanation: if we experience binding “oughts,” naturalism’s reduction of morality to preference or evolutionary byproduct under-explains the phenomenology. William Lane Craig’s moral argument materials ([[S-032|Moral Arguments for God’s Existence]], [[S-002|Craig, Reasonable Faith]]) and Keller’s pastoral-philosophical treatment ([[S-011|Keller, Reason for God]]) press a similar claim: objective moral values and duties fit classical theism better than atheistic naturalism.

This argument is philosophical, not a proof from Scripture quotations alone. It supports [[C-001|God exists]] first; any bridge toward [[C-002|God of the Bible]] (biblical God) is secondary and contested by [[A-ANTI-003|Biblical God fails moral empirical tests]]. Under symmetry, if Anti uses moral horror as evidence *against* theism ([[S-060|SEP Problem of Evil]], [[S-065|Ehrman, God’s Problem]]), Pro may use moral obligation as evidence *for* a moral lawgiver — both are moral data, differently interpreted.

The argument fails if objective morals are shown unnecessary, or if theism’s own evil problem ([[A-ANTI-002|Classical theism fails under scrutiny]]) cancels the moral uplift without remainder. It does not by itself identify Yahweh or settle Scripture’s historical claims.
