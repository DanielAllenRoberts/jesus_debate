# A-PRO-012 — Allison modest historicity favors resurrection hypothesis

**In short:** This argument supports [[C-006|Resurrection]] using Dale Allison’s cautious historiography: even when you refuse apologetic overclaim, empty-tomb and appearance traditions still raise resurrection relative to pure vision-legend models. “Modest” means: don’t pretend certainty; still weigh hypotheses.

## Claim targeted

[[C-006|Resurrection]] — Jesus rose bodily from the dead.


## Thesis

Even under cautious critical historiography that refuses apologetic overclaim, historically defensible empty-tomb and appearance traditions raise the resurrection hypothesis relative to pure vision-legend stacks.

## Argument

Dale Allison’s major study ([[S-049|Allison, Resurrection of Jesus]]) is neither a Habermas-style consensus ledger nor a Lüdemann dismissal. It interrogates formulae, appearances, and Friday/Sunday tomb traditions with unusual fairness, finding relatively stronger historical footing for empty-tomb and certain appearance traditions while cataloging tenuous arguments on *both* apologetic and skeptical sides. Pro uses Allison as a **critical-leaning** secondary source that still pressures [[A-ANTI-001|Vision-origin naturalistic Easter]]: if empty tomb and key appearances survive modest historical tests, grief-vision-plus-late-legend must do more work.

Philosophical encyclopedias ([[S-094|IEP Resurrection]], [[S-095|SEP Afterlife]]) clarify that “bodily resurrection” claims face identity puzzles under materialism; Pro notes these are metaphysical follow-ons, not historical erasers of the Easter data. The historical claim remains comparative: given early creed ([[S-037|Habermas, Experiences of the Risen Jesus]]), crucifixion ([[C-005|Crucifixion]] Strengthened), and Allison-level tomb/appearance residual, [[C-006|Resurrection]] is more than “legend only,” even if not Proven.

This complements [[A-PRO-004|Minimal facts resurrection case]] and [[A-PRO-011|Empty tomb + early creed as resurrection hinges]] while respecting [[SC-001|Challenge to S-047 minimal facts consensus rhetoric]]’s limit on consensus-percentage rhetoric.
