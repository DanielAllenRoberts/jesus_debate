# A-PRO-013 — Cultic devotion shows early divine honor

**In short:** This argument supports [[C-003|Jesus Son of God died for sins]]. Earliest recoverable Christian practice gave Jesus **cultic devotion**—honor and worship-patterns normally reserved for God—suggesting exalted identity at the movement’s origin, not only after Nicaea.

## Claim targeted

[[C-003|Jesus Son of God died for sins]] — Jesus is the divine Son of God, and his death saves people from sin.


## Thesis

Earliest recoverable Christian practice treats Jesus as rightful recipient of honor normally reserved for God, placing exalted identity at the movement’s origin rather than only at fourth-century councils.

## Argument

Hurtado’s clarifying essay ([[S-096|Hurtado, Early High Christology clarifying]]) and BibleInterp survey ([[S-099|Hurtado, Devotion to Jesus]]) emphasize *devotional practice* — invocation, worship patterns, dyadic God-and-Lord structure — already assumed in Pauline letters of the 50s and treated as traditional. That undercuts slow Hellenization models and strengthens [[A-PRO-008|Early creed + high Christology at origins]] against [[A-ANTI-004|Christology developed after Jesus]]’s “developed after Jesus” maximal reading.

Pro still grants [[R-009|Early Christology as post-Easter]]’s distinction: early church devotion can be post-Easter relative to the historical Jesus. The R02 advance is narrower: [[C-A003|Nicene Christianity later construction]]’s “later Nicene construction” cannot mean “no early divine honor.” Councils ([[S-089|First Council of Nicaea]], [[S-090|First Council of Constantinople / Creed]]) define metaphysics; they do not originate the practice of treating Jesus as Kyrios beside God.

Combined with early creed salvific death language ([[S-037|Habermas, Experiences of the Risen Jesus]]), Pro claims continuity of *referent* for [[C-003|Jesus Son of God died for sins]] sufficient to keep Anti from Strengthening [[C-A003|Nicene Christianity later construction]] into a Foundation-level defeat of the stack.
