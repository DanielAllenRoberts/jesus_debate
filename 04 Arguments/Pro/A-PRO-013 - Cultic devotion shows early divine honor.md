# A-PRO-013 — Cultic devotion shows early divine honor

## Claim targeted

[[C-003]]

Opposing: [[C-A003]]

## Thesis

Earliest recoverable Christian practice treats Jesus as rightful recipient of honor normally reserved for God, placing exalted identity at the movement’s origin rather than only at fourth-century councils.

## Argument

Hurtado’s clarifying essay ([[S-096]]) and BibleInterp survey ([[S-099]]) emphasize *devotional practice* — invocation, worship patterns, dyadic God-and-Lord structure — already assumed in Pauline letters of the 50s and treated as traditional. That undercuts slow Hellenization models and strengthens [[A-PRO-008]] against [[A-ANTI-004]]’s “developed after Jesus” maximal reading.

Pro still grants [[R-009]]’s distinction: early church devotion can be post-Easter relative to the historical Jesus. The R02 advance is narrower: [[C-A003]]’s “later Nicene construction” cannot mean “no early divine honor.” Councils ([[S-089]], [[S-090]]) define metaphysics; they do not originate the practice of treating Jesus as Kyrios beside God.

Combined with early creed salvific death language ([[S-037]]), Pro claims continuity of *referent* for [[C-003]] sufficient to keep Anti from Strengthening [[C-A003]] into a Foundation-level defeat of the stack.
