# A-PRO-010 — Crucifixion under Pilate among firmest HJ facts

**In short:** This argument supports [[C-005|Crucifixion]]. Crucifixion under Pontius Pilate is treated as among the firmest conclusions in historical Jesus research—even when scholars disagree about theology. It is a historical baseline for atonement and resurrection debates, not a proof of those doctrines.

## Claim targeted

[[C-005|Crucifixion]] — Jesus was crucified under Roman authority (early 30s CE).


## Thesis

That Jesus was crucified under Pontius Pilate is among the firmest conclusions of historical Jesus research, independently of Christian theological claims about atonement or resurrection.

## Argument

Multiple streams converge. Christian proclamation treats crucifixion as public shame rather than inventible glory, which critical scholars often read as criterion-of-embarrassment support. Non-Christian attestation includes Tacitus ([[S-008|Tacitus, Annals 15.44]]) naming execution under Pilate, and Josephan references ([[S-009|Josephus, Antiquities (Jesus & James)]], [[S-046|Josephus Testimonium Flavianum text]]) locating Jesus’ death in the Pilate period. Mainstream reconstructions by Meier ([[S-040|Meier, A Marginal Jew Vol. 1]]), Sanders ([[S-044|Sanders, Historical Figure of Jesus]]), and even Crossan ([[S-045|Crossan, Jesus: A Revolutionary Biography]]) presuppose crucifixion as historical baseline while disagreeing about meaning.

Habermas’s minimal-facts literature ([[S-047|Habermas, Minimal Facts approach]], [[S-004|Habermas & Licona, Case for the Resurrection]]) treats death by crucifixion as one of the least disputed data points even among scholars skeptical of resurrection. Van Voorst’s survey of non-Christian evidence ([[S-042|Van Voorst, Jesus Outside the NT]]) and Josephus-critical literature ([[S-041|Jesus in Josephus (modest proposal)]], [[S-043|Josephus on Jesus (Testimonium controversy)]]) are relevant insofar as they assess independent attestation, not Gospel harmonization.

Anti has not filed a dedicated existence-denying or crucifixion-denying argument. Indirect pressure comes from Crossan’s passion-as-later-development skepticism ([[S-078|Crossan, Who Killed Jesus?]]) inside [[A-ANTI-001|Vision-origin naturalistic Easter]] / [[A-ANTI-006|Gospel distance allows legendary embossing]]. Pro’s reply: skepticism about *narrative embroidery* of the passion is not equivalent to skepticism that a crucifixion occurred. Under [[Constitution]] §1, Anti must cite sources that actually deny the crucifixion event if that is the claim; chronological legendary embossing of details does not by itself refute [[C-005|Crucifixion]].

This claim is load-bearing for [[C-006|Resurrection]] and [[C-003|Jesus Son of God died for sins]]: resurrection and atonement claims require a real death. Strengthening [[C-005|Crucifixion]] does not settle those later claims.
