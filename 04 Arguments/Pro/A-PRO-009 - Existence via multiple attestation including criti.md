# A-PRO-009 — Existence via multiple attestation including critics

**In short:** This argument supports [[C-004|Historical Jesus]]. Multiple streams—Christian writings plus non-Christian notices (e.g., Josephus, Tacitus)—and even critical scholars who reject Christianity still affirm Jesus existed. **Multiple attestation** = independent sources saying related things raises historical confidence.

## Claim targeted

[[C-004|Historical Jesus]] — Jesus of Nazareth was a real person in first-century Judea/Galilee.


## Thesis

Christian streams plus Josephus/Tacitus; critics also affirm historicity.

## Argument

## Argument

Jesus’ existence is multiply attested across Christian traditions and in non-Christian notices: Tacitus ([[S-008|Tacitus, Annals 15.44]]), Josephus ([[S-009|Josephus, Antiquities (Jesus & James)]], [[S-046|Josephus Testimonium Flavianum text]]), and critical scholarship affirming historicity ([[S-007|Ehrman, Did Jesus Exist?]], [[S-040|Meier, A Marginal Jew Vol. 1]], [[S-042|Van Voorst, Jesus Outside the NT]], [[S-044|Sanders, Historical Figure of Jesus]], [[S-074|Casey, Jesus: Evidence and Argument]]). Mythicist alternatives ([[S-073|Christ myth theory (article)]], [[S-075|Maccoby, Mythmaker]]) remain minority positions; Anti’s own [[A-ANTI-007|Mythicism is fringe — do not overclaim]] declines to rest on them.

This argument overlaps [[A-PRO-003|Historical Jesus and crucifixion historically secure]] and feeds [[C-004|Historical Jesus]]. It does not establish resurrection or divinity. Its scoring relevance is baseline historicity.
