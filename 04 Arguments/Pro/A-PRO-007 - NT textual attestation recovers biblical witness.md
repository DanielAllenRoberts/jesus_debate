# A-PRO-007 — NT textual attestation recovers biblical witness

**In short:** This argument supports [[C-002|God of the Bible]] on the narrow point of **textual recoverability**: we have enough New Testament manuscripts to reconstruct, with high confidence, what the authors wrote. That is not the same as proving the content is true—only that “the biblical witness” isn’t lost to copyist chaos.

## Claim targeted

[[C-002|God of the Bible]] — If God exists, it is the God revealed in the Bible—not just any deity.


## Thesis

Manuscript density supports recoverable biblical witness for C-002.

## Argument

## Argument

Compared with many ancient works, the New Testament has early and abundant manuscript attestation ([[S-034|Bruce, NT Documents Reliable?]], [[S-035|Wallace interview on NT manuscripts]], [[S-036|Metzger/Ehrman, Text of the NT]]). Pro argues this supports recovering a stable biblical witness sufficient for historical and theological debate about what early Christians claimed — a prerequisite for identifying the God of [[C-002|God of the Bible]] with the Creator of [[C-001|God exists]].

Anti’s [[R-010|Text recovery vs doctrinal truth]] and [[S-026|Ehrman, Misquoting Jesus]] correctly note that textual recovery ≠ truth of doctrine, and variants exist. Pro grants the distinction: this argument secures *access to the witness*, not moral vindication of every biblical depiction (that fight is [[A-ANTI-003|Biblical God fails moral empirical tests]] vs theodicy/moral replies). Without recoverable text, [[C-002|God of the Bible]] becomes untestable; with it, the moral/historical critiques can at least target the right referent.
