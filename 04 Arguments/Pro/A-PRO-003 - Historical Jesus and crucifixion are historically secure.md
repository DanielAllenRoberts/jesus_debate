# A-PRO-003 — Historical Jesus and crucifixion are historically secure

**In short:** This argument supports [[C-004|Historical Jesus]] (and the crucifixion baseline). Using ordinary history tools—multiple independent sources, even hostile ones, and cultural fit—Jesus of Nazareth and his Roman execution are treated as historically secure. This is about *existence and death*, not yet resurrection or divinity.

## Claim targeted

[[C-004|Historical Jesus]] — Jesus of Nazareth was a real person in first-century Judea/Galilee.


## Thesis

Using standard historical criteria — multiple attestation, enemy attestation, and contextual coherence — Jesus of Nazareth is a real first-century figure, and his crucifixion under Roman authority is among the most secure facts about him.

## Argument

Bart Ehrman’s *Did Jesus Exist?* ([[S-007|Ehrman, Did Jesus Exist?]]) is cited here not as Christian apologetics but as a critical scholar’s case that mythicism fails ordinary historical method. Pro treats that as significant under symmetry: a scholar often used by Anti elsewhere ([[S-025|Ehrman, How Jesus Became God]], [[S-026|Ehrman, Misquoting Jesus]], [[S-066|Ehrman, Jesus Interrupted]]) affirms the historicity baseline. Tacitus, *Annals* 15.44 ([[S-008|Tacitus, Annals 15.44]]), reports Christus executed under Pontius Pilate in a hostile Roman context. Josephus materials ([[S-009|Josephus, Antiquities (Jesus & James)]], [[S-046|Josephus Testimonium Flavianum text]], with critical discussion in [[S-041|Jesus in Josephus (modest proposal)]], [[S-043|Josephus on Jesus (Testimonium controversy)]]) independently place Jesus and James in first-century Judea, even where Testimonium wording is debated.

Meier ([[S-040|Meier, A Marginal Jew Vol. 1]]), Sanders ([[S-044|Sanders, Historical Figure of Jesus]]), and Van Voorst ([[S-042|Van Voorst, Jesus Outside the NT]]) represent mainstream historical Jesus scholarship treating existence and crucifixion as secure starting points for reconstruction. Crossan’s revolutionary biography ([[S-045|Crossan, Jesus: A Revolutionary Biography]]) differs theologically and methodologically from evangelical reconstructions but still presupposes a historical crucified Jesus. Wikipedia’s historicity overview ([[S-072|Historicity of Jesus (article)]]) and mythicism overview ([[S-073|Christ myth theory (article)]]) are tertiary pointers only; the load-bearing sources are the primary/secondary works above.

Anti’s own [[A-ANTI-007|Mythicism is fringe — do not overclaim]] explicitly marks mythicism fringe and refuses overclaim. That does not prove every Pro reconstruction of Jesus’ teaching, but it does mean the existence fight is not the main Anti lane. Crucifixion detail is expanded in [[A-PRO-010|Crucifixion under Pilate among firmest HJ facts]]. The present argument’s job for the truth stack is modest and foundational: without [[C-004|Historical Jesus]]/[[C-005|Crucifixion]], later claims about resurrection and divine Sonship have no historical subject.
