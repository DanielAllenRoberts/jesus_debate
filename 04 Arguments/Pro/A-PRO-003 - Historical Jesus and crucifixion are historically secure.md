# A-PRO-003 — Historical Jesus and crucifixion are historically secure

## Claim targeted

[[C-004]]

Related: [[C-005]]

## Thesis

Using standard historical criteria — multiple attestation, enemy attestation, and contextual coherence — Jesus of Nazareth is a real first-century figure, and his crucifixion under Roman authority is among the most secure facts about him.

## Argument

Bart Ehrman’s *Did Jesus Exist?* ([[S-007]]) is cited here not as Christian apologetics but as a critical scholar’s case that mythicism fails ordinary historical method. Pro treats that as significant under symmetry: a scholar often used by Anti elsewhere ([[S-025]], [[S-026]], [[S-066]]) affirms the historicity baseline. Tacitus, *Annals* 15.44 ([[S-008]]), reports Christus executed under Pontius Pilate in a hostile Roman context. Josephus materials ([[S-009]], [[S-046]], with critical discussion in [[S-041]], [[S-043]]) independently place Jesus and James in first-century Judea, even where Testimonium wording is debated.

Meier ([[S-040]]), Sanders ([[S-044]]), and Van Voorst ([[S-042]]) represent mainstream historical Jesus scholarship treating existence and crucifixion as secure starting points for reconstruction. Crossan’s revolutionary biography ([[S-045]]) differs theologically and methodologically from evangelical reconstructions but still presupposes a historical crucified Jesus. Wikipedia’s historicity overview ([[S-072]]) and mythicism overview ([[S-073]]) are tertiary pointers only; the load-bearing sources are the primary/secondary works above.

Anti’s own [[A-ANTI-007]] explicitly marks mythicism fringe and refuses overclaim. That does not prove every Pro reconstruction of Jesus’ teaching, but it does mean the existence fight is not the main Anti lane. Crucifixion detail is expanded in [[A-PRO-010]]. The present argument’s job for the truth stack is modest and foundational: without [[C-004]]/[[C-005]], later claims about resurrection and divine Sonship have no historical subject.
