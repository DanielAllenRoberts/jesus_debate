# A-PRO-006 — Aquinas kalam fine-tuning cumulative case

**In short:** This argument supports [[C-001|God exists]] by stacking classical natural-theology moves: contingency (why contingent reality?), kalām (began-to-exist needs cause), and fine-tuning. A **cumulative case** means no single proof has to do all the work—the combination is supposed to make a personal Creator more probable.

## Claim targeted

[[C-001|God exists]] — A real, personal creator God exists (classical theism).

Opposing Anti claim: [[C-A002|No classical theistic God]] — There is no classical personal creator God of the kind Pro needs.


## Thesis

Contingency, kalām, and fine-tuning jointly favor a personal creator.

## Argument

## Argument

This note packages three planks into one cumulative case for [[C-001|God exists]]. Aquinas’s contingency/causal ways ([[S-030|Aquinas, Summa Five Ways]]) ask why contingent reality exists. Kalām presentations ([[S-031|Craig, Kalam Argument]], [[S-002|Craig, Reasonable Faith]]) add a beginning-sensitive causal principle. Fine-tuning literature ([[S-033|Fine-Tuning (resource)]], [[S-014|SEP Teleological Arguments]], [[S-012|Swinburne, Existence of God]]) adds life-permitting structure as further data. Swinburne’s confirmation framework ([[S-012|Swinburne, Existence of God]]) licenses combining independent lines if they are not fully redundant.

Anti replies via Oppy ([[S-022|Oppy, Arguing about Gods]]), Mackie ([[S-021|Mackie, Miracle of Theism]]), and evil/hiddenness ([[S-060|SEP Problem of Evil]]–[[S-063|Schellenberg, Divine Hiddenness]]) — see [[R-012|Cumulative case faces defeaters]] and [[R-008|Cosmology underdetermines classical God]]. Pro’s claim is not that each plank is Proven, but that together they outweigh a flat naturalist shrug *before* evil is counted; after evil is counted, the Foundation fight remains comparative and Contested.
