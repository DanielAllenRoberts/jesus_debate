# A-ANTI-001 — Vision-origin naturalistic Easter

## Claim targeted

[[C-A001]]

Opposing Pro claim: [[C-006]]

## Thesis

Easter faith originated in grief-driven visionary experiences of Jesus’ followers; empty-tomb narratives are secondary legendary developments and do not historically establish bodily resurrection.

## Argument

Gerd Lüdemann’s historical reconstruction ([[S-027]], popularized pathway in [[S-077]]) locates the rise of resurrection belief in psychological and social dynamics after Jesus’ traumatic death: mourning, guilt, and visionary experiences that were interpreted within Jewish symbolic resources. On this model, the disciples’ sincere conviction that they “saw” Jesus is historically real as *experience*, but the experience does not entail a reanimated corpse. Anti therefore grants appearance *reports* while denying Pro’s metaphysical inference.

Empty tomb is treated as later legend. If the earliest recoverable core is visionary proclamation rather than verified tomb inspection, then Habermas/Craig empty-tomb hinges ([[S-048]], [[A-PRO-011]]) are not load-bearing for the origin of faith. Crossan’s passion and burial skepticism ([[S-078]]) reinforces that the narrative packaging of death, burial, and tomb discovery is theologically shaped and historically late relative to the first experiences.

Hume’s classic essay on miracles ([[S-023]]) supplies the epistemic frame: miracle testimony must overcome the prior improbability of violating natural regularities. Even if naturalistic explanations are individually imperfect, the miracle hypothesis carries a higher burden. Ehrman’s broader exaltation narrative ([[S-025]]) situates resurrection belief inside developing christological interpretation rather than as a brute physical event forcing orthodoxy. Debate media ([[S-082]], [[S-086]]) illustrate contemporary naturalistic and critical alternatives but are secondary to Lüdemann/Crossan/Hume for scoring.

This argument does not require Jesus-mythicism; it presupposes a historical crucified Jesus ([[A-ANTI-007]]). The claim is only that [[C-006]] fails as historical inference. If Pro shows that early creed burial language plus Jerusalem proclamation make occupied-tomb silence historically incredible, this vision-first model is pressured.
