# A-ANTI-027 — Earliness of belief ≠ veridicality of miracle

## Explain for the reader

Even if Easter faith is extremely early, **how soon people believed something is not how likely a miracle is**. Humean and encyclopedia-level miracle epistemology still bound the inference from early creed to bodily resurrection.

## Claim targeted

[[C-A001|No resurrection]] — *keeps Strengthened status as epistemic mirror*

## Related claims

- Opposing: [[C-006|Resurrection]]
- Sibling: [[A-ANTI-026|Early creed ≠ empty tomb]] · [[A-ANTI-010|Humean miracle epistemology]] · [[A-ANTI-014|Miracle priors swamp Swinburne]]

## Thesis

Chronological proximity reduces some legendary-growth stories but does not cancel the prior improbability of resurrection miracles. Pro’s creed-window deepen is historically interesting and still insufficient for Proven or for collapsing the Anti Strengthened mirror.

## Argument

1. **Category error.** “Early sincere belief that X” ≠ “X occurred,” especially for maximally unusual X ([[S-101|SEP Miracles]]; [[S-023|Hume]]).
2. **Ehrman as witness against the leap.** The same critical scholar who grants early tradition ([[S-133|Ehrman]]) does not treat that as establishing classical bodily resurrection — showing earliness and veridicality come apart in competent historiography.
3. **Apologetic packaging.** Popular creed-to-case articles ([[S-136|CrossExamined]]) often slide from dating to demonstration; [[SC-014|SC-014]] upholds the limit.
4. **Allison/Habermas tension.** Even Pro-friendly exchanges ([[S-135|Habermas on Allison]]) leave apparition alternatives and modest historical results on the table — not a closed miracle proof.
5. **Score ask:** Confirm dual Easter Strengthened mirrors; probability **52.0%** unchanged.
