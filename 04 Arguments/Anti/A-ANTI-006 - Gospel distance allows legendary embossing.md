# A-ANTI-006 — Gospel distance allows legendary embossing

**In short:** This argument targets [[C-006|Resurrection]]. If the finished Gospels are typically dated after 70 CE, decades separate Jesus’ death from narrative fixation—time enough for theological embossing of passion, burial, and empty-tomb stories. Embossing = legendary/theological polishing of memory.

## Claim targeted

[[C-006|Resurrection]] — Jesus rose bodily from the dead.


## Thesis

Standard critical dating of the canonical Gospels after 70 CE leaves decades between Jesus’ death and narrative fixation, enough time for theological embossing of passion, burial, and empty-tomb stories that Pro treats as historical hinges.

## Argument

Bart Ehrman’s dating argument ([[S-076|Why Date the Gospels after 70 CE?]]) represents a widely taught critical position: Mark after the Jewish War, Matthew/Luke later, John later still. Anti does not need every scholar to agree on exact years; the relevant point is *decades*, not stenographic reportage. Within that window, oral tradition, community conflict, and christological development can shape stories. Ehrman’s *Jesus, Interrupted* ([[S-066|Ehrman, Jesus Interrupted]]) catalogs contradictions and theological agendas across Gospels that resist simple harmonization into one camera-ready passion chronology.

Crossan’s *Who Killed Jesus?* ([[S-078|Crossan, Who Killed Jesus?]]) argues that passion materials are heavily theologized and historically problematic, including burial traditions Pro needs for empty-tomb arguments ([[A-PRO-011|Empty tomb + early creed as resurrection hinges]]). Synoptic relationship debates ([[S-067|Did Gospel authors know each other?]], [[S-068|Case Against Q / Bible Odyssey]]) matter because literary dependence means multiple Gospels are not independent eyewitness channels in the apologetic sense. If Matthew and Luke know Mark, “multiple attestation” of tomb details is weakened.

This argument attacks Pro’s path from Gospel narrative detail to [[C-006|Resurrection]]. It does not by itself refute every early-creed argument in [[A-PRO-004|Minimal facts resurrection case]] / [[A-PRO-011|Empty tomb + early creed as resurrection hinges]], because Paul predates the finished Gospels. Anti therefore pairs this note with [[A-ANTI-001|Vision-origin naturalistic Easter]]: creed may preserve early *belief*, while Gospels emboss *narrative scenery*. Pro must not conflate early proclamation with late narrative reliability.
