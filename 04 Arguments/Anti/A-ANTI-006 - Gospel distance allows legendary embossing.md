# A-ANTI-006 — Gospel distance allows legendary embossing

## Claim targeted

[[C-006]]

Related opposing: [[C-A001]]

## Thesis

Standard critical dating of the canonical Gospels after 70 CE leaves decades between Jesus’ death and narrative fixation, enough time for theological embossing of passion, burial, and empty-tomb stories that Pro treats as historical hinges.

## Argument

Bart Ehrman’s dating argument ([[S-076]]) represents a widely taught critical position: Mark after the Jewish War, Matthew/Luke later, John later still. Anti does not need every scholar to agree on exact years; the relevant point is *decades*, not stenographic reportage. Within that window, oral tradition, community conflict, and christological development can shape stories. Ehrman’s *Jesus, Interrupted* ([[S-066]]) catalogs contradictions and theological agendas across Gospels that resist simple harmonization into one camera-ready passion chronology.

Crossan’s *Who Killed Jesus?* ([[S-078]]) argues that passion materials are heavily theologized and historically problematic, including burial traditions Pro needs for empty-tomb arguments ([[A-PRO-011]]). Synoptic relationship debates ([[S-067]], [[S-068]]) matter because literary dependence means multiple Gospels are not independent eyewitness channels in the apologetic sense. If Matthew and Luke know Mark, “multiple attestation” of tomb details is weakened.

This argument attacks Pro’s path from Gospel narrative detail to [[C-006]]. It does not by itself refute every early-creed argument in [[A-PRO-004]] / [[A-PRO-011]], because Paul predates the finished Gospels. Anti therefore pairs this note with [[A-ANTI-001]]: creed may preserve early *belief*, while Gospels emboss *narrative scenery*. Pro must not conflate early proclamation with late narrative reliability.
