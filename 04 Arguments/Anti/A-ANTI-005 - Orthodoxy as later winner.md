# A-ANTI-005 — Orthodoxy as later winner

## Claim targeted

[[C-A003]]

Opposing Pro claim: [[C-003]]

## Thesis

Earliest Christianity was pluriform; what later counts as orthodoxy is a historical winner among competing Christianities, not a simple transcription of Jesus’ original teaching.

## Argument

Walter Bauer’s classic thesis ([[S-029]]) argues that in several regions forms later labeled heretical were early and strong, while “orthodoxy” consolidated with institutional power, especially Roman influence. Elaine Pagels’s *The Gnostic Gospels* ([[S-028]]) popularizes Nag Hammadi evidence for alternative Christianities with different cosmologies and christs. Even if specialists revise Bauer’s map, the broader point remains: diversity is early; singularity is later.

Nicaea and Constantinople ([[S-089]], [[S-090]]) are political-theological settlements. Anti uses them as terminus markers: the Pro stack’s classical Son/atonement package, in its finished creedal form, is downstream of contest. Ehrman’s textual and contradiction work ([[S-026]], [[S-066]]) further undermines naive appeals to a single stable biblical voice delivering [[C-003]] untouched.

Pro’s early creed and Hurtado devotion ([[A-PRO-008]]) show an influential stream early; they do not show it was the only stream or that Nicene metaphysics was present. Anti’s claim for [[C-A003]] is that “later construction” is the right description for the finished orthodox package relative to the messy first centuries.
