# A-ANTI-005 — Orthodoxy as later winner

**In short:** This argument supports [[C-A003|Nicene Christianity later construction]]. Earliest Christianity was many movements (**pluriform**); “orthodoxy” is the historical winner, not a simple tape-recording of Jesus’ teaching. Winning a fourth-century creed fight is not the same as preserving pristine origins.

## Claim targeted

[[C-A003|Nicene Christianity later construction]] — Nicene-style orthodox Christianity was largely built later, not simply taught by the historical Jesus.

Opposing Pro claim: [[C-003|Jesus Son of God died for sins]] — Jesus is the divine Son of God, and his death saves people from sin.


## Thesis

Earliest Christianity was pluriform; what later counts as orthodoxy is a historical winner among competing Christianities, not a simple transcription of Jesus’ original teaching.

## Argument

Walter Bauer’s classic thesis ([[S-029|Bauer, Orthodoxy and Heresy]]) argues that in several regions forms later labeled heretical were early and strong, while “orthodoxy” consolidated with institutional power, especially Roman influence. Elaine Pagels’s *The Gnostic Gospels* ([[S-028|Pagels, Gnostic Gospels]]) popularizes Nag Hammadi evidence for alternative Christianities with different cosmologies and christs. Even if specialists revise Bauer’s map, the broader point remains: diversity is early; singularity is later.

Nicaea and Constantinople ([[S-089|First Council of Nicaea]], [[S-090|First Council of Constantinople / Creed]]) are political-theological settlements. Anti uses them as terminus markers: the Pro stack’s classical Son/atonement package, in its finished creedal form, is downstream of contest. Ehrman’s textual and contradiction work ([[S-026|Ehrman, Misquoting Jesus]], [[S-066|Ehrman, Jesus Interrupted]]) further undermines naive appeals to a single stable biblical voice delivering [[C-003|Jesus Son of God died for sins]] untouched.

Pro’s early creed and Hurtado devotion ([[A-PRO-008|Early creed + high Christology at origins]]) show an influential stream early; they do not show it was the only stream or that Nicene metaphysics was present. Anti’s claim for [[C-A003|Nicene Christianity later construction]] is that “later construction” is the right description for the finished orthodox package relative to the messy first centuries.
