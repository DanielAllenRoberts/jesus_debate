# A-ANTI-003 — Biblical God fails moral empirical tests

## Claim targeted

[[C-002]]

Related Anti claim: [[C-A002]]

## Thesis

Even if a deity existed, the biblical God fails moral and empirical tests.

## Argument

## Argument

Even if some deity existed ([[C-001]] fight unsettled), the God depicted in the Bible fails moral and empirical tests central to classical Christian theism. Ehrman’s *God’s Problem* ([[S-065]]) argues biblical responses to suffering are inadequate. Broader evil literature ([[S-060]], [[S-061]]) applies with special force when the deity is identified with Scriptural portrayals of command, judgment, and history. Popular critiques ([[S-016]], [[S-017]], [[S-018]], [[S-084]], [[S-092]]) amplify the moral indictment; Neutrals should prefer [[S-065]]/SEP-level sources over polemic bestsellers ([[SC-002]], [[R-011]]).

Empirical-historical attacks on biblical narratives (where cited carefully) further detach “Creator” from “biblical God,” threatening [[C-002]] even if [[C-001]] somehow held. Pro’s textual recovery ([[A-PRO-007]]) does not answer this moral gap ([[R-010]]).
