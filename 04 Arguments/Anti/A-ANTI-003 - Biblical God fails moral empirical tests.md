# A-ANTI-003 — Biblical God fails moral empirical tests

**In short:** This argument targets [[C-002|God of the Bible]]. Even if some deity existed, the biblical God’s moral record and empirical claims fail basic tests. This can weaken “biblical identification” even if bare theism ([[C-001|God exists]]) remains open.

## Claim targeted

[[C-002|God of the Bible]] — If God exists, it is the God revealed in the Bible—not just any deity.


## Thesis

Even if a deity existed, the biblical God fails moral and empirical tests.

## Argument

## Argument

Even if some deity existed ([[C-001|God exists]] fight unsettled), the God depicted in the Bible fails moral and empirical tests central to classical Christian theism. Ehrman’s *God’s Problem* ([[S-065|Ehrman, God’s Problem]]) argues biblical responses to suffering are inadequate. Broader evil literature ([[S-060|SEP Problem of Evil]], [[S-061|Mackie, Evil and Omnipotence]]) applies with special force when the deity is identified with Scriptural portrayals of command, judgment, and history. Popular critiques ([[S-016|Dawkins, God Delusion]], [[S-017|Hitchens, God Is Not Great]], [[S-018|Harris, Letter to a Christian Nation]], [[S-084|Nothing Fails Like Bible History Ep. 1]], [[S-092|Greatest Lie Ever Told]]) amplify the moral indictment; Neutrals should prefer [[S-065|Ehrman, God’s Problem]]/SEP-level sources over polemic bestsellers ([[SC-002|Challenge to S-016 as Foundation-grade philosophy]], [[R-011|Moral critique citation discipline]]).

Empirical-historical attacks on biblical narratives (where cited carefully) further detach “Creator” from “biblical God,” threatening [[C-002|God of the Bible]] even if [[C-001|God exists]] somehow held. Pro’s textual recovery ([[A-PRO-007|NT textual attestation recovers biblical witness]]) does not answer this moral gap ([[R-010|Text recovery vs doctrinal truth]]).
