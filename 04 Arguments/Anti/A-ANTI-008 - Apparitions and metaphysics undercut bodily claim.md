# A-ANTI-008 — Apparitions and metaphysics undercut bodily claim

**In short:** This argument supports [[C-A001|Resurrection did not occur]]. Even sincere early “encounters” can be apparitions/visions, and philosophy raises hard problems for bodily resurrection identity. Historical residues of Easter belief don’t automatically equal a physical rising.

## Claim targeted

[[C-A001|Resurrection did not occur]] — Jesus did not rise from the dead as classical Christianity claims.


## Thesis

Even if early Christians sincerely reported encounters with Jesus, apparition/vision categories plus philosophical problems for bodily resurrection keep a physical rising historically and metaphysically underdetermined.

## Argument

Building on Lüdemann ([[S-027|Lüdemann, Resurrection of Jesus]]) and Hume ([[S-023|Hume, Of Miracles]]), Anti adds encyclopedia-level framing: IEP Resurrection ([[S-094|IEP Resurrection]]) and SEP Afterlife ([[S-095|SEP Afterlife]]) show that “bodily resurrection” is philosophically fraught under materialism (identity across death). Historical reports of seeing Jesus are compatible with visionary/religious experience without a reanimated corpse.

Allison ([[S-049|Allison, Resurrection of Jesus]]) — used by Pro in [[A-PRO-012|Allison modest historicity favors resurrection hypothesis]] — also discusses apparition correlations and critiques tenuous apologetic arguments. Anti steelmans Allison’s caution: modest historical residues do not compel [[C-006|Resurrection]]. Gospel embossing ([[A-ANTI-006|Gospel distance allows legendary embossing]], [[S-076|Why Date the Gospels after 70 CE?]]) still explains narrative bodily details. Net: [[C-A001|Resurrection did not occur]] remains the better total explanation when miracle priors ([[S-023|Hume, Of Miracles]]) are included.
