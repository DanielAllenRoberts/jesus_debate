# A-ANTI-002 — Classical theism fails under scrutiny

## Claim targeted

[[C-A002]]

Opposing Pro claim: [[C-001]]

## Thesis

Classical theistic proofs do not establish an omnipotent omnibenevolent Creator; the problem of evil and divine hiddenness make such a God improbable on the total evidence.

## Argument

J. L. Mackie’s “Evil and Omnipotence” ([[S-061]]) and *The Miracle of Theism* ([[S-021]]) argue that the coexistence of God and evil is either logically inconsistent or, at minimum, that evil drastically lowers theism’s probability. The Stanford Encyclopedia entry on the problem of evil ([[S-060]]) surveys logical and evidential forms; Anti leans on the evidential form: the quantity and kinds of suffering we observe are surprising on classical theism, less surprising on naturalism. Schellenberg’s divine hiddenness argument ([[S-063]], SEP overview [[S-062]]) adds that nonresistant nonbelief is itself evidence against a perfectly loving God who seeks relationship.

Graham Oppy’s systematic *Arguing about Gods* ([[S-022]]) provides a broad critical toolkit against cosmological and teleological proofs Pro relies on in [[A-PRO-001]] and [[A-PRO-006]]. Popular New Atheist volumes ([[S-016]], [[S-024]]) are secondary rhetorical amplifiers, not the scholarly spine; Neutrals should weight Mackie/Oppy/SEP/Schellenberg higher than Dawkins/Stenger for Foundation scoring.

Anti’s claim is comparative: even if contingency or fine-tuning give *some* prior lift to “a cause” or “a designer,” that lift does not uniquely yield classical theism’s moral attributes, and evil/hiddenness are powerful down-weights. Pro’s cumulative case must not stop at cosmic origins while ignoring the moral data. Under symmetry, if unexplained cosmic order counts for theism, unexplained horrific suffering counts against it.

This argument targets [[C-001]] / classical God. Separate moral critiques of the *biblical* deity are in [[A-ANTI-003]] on [[C-002]].
