# A-ANTI-004 — Christology developed after Jesus

**In short:** This argument supports [[C-A003|Nicene Christianity later construction]] against [[C-003|Jesus Son of God died for sins]]. Jesus did not teach Nicene-level divine identity; exaltation/incarnation beliefs developed after his death as followers reinterpreted him. **Exaltation Christology** = Jesus became/ was declared exalted after death/resurrection proclamation.

## Claim targeted

[[C-A003|Nicene Christianity later construction]] — Nicene-style orthodox Christianity was largely built later, not simply taught by the historical Jesus.

Opposing Pro claim: [[C-003|Jesus Son of God died for sins]] — Jesus is the divine Son of God, and his death saves people from sin.


## Thesis

Jesus did not teach Nicene-level divine identity; exaltation and incarnation Christologies developed after his death as the movement reinterpreted him.

## Argument

Bart Ehrman’s *How Jesus Became God* ([[S-025|Ehrman, How Jesus Became God]]) traces a developmental arc: followers exalt the crucified Jesus; later reflection intensifies divine language; incarnation theology matures in ways foreign to Jesus’ own historical proclamation. Anti’s point is historical discontinuity between the Galilean teacher and the later ontological Son of [[C-003|Jesus Son of God died for sins]]. Early high devotion, even if granted from Hurtado ([[S-039|Hurtado, Early High Christology / Lord Jesus Christ]]), can still be *post-Easter invention* relative to Jesus himself.

Council history ([[S-089|First Council of Nicaea]], [[S-090|First Council of Constantinople / Creed]]) shows fourth-century settlement of contested metaphysical formulas. That settlement is evidence of controversy, not of first-generation consensus on homoousios. Anti does not claim “no early exaltation”; it claims Pro overreads early exaltation as if it already were the full Pro truth-stack identity (divine Son whose death atones in classical Christian sense).

This pairs with [[A-ANTI-005|Orthodoxy as later winner]]: development plus pluriform competition explain orthodoxy’s later shape. If Pro shows Jesus himself claimed the relevant identity with historically secure sayings, this argument weakens. If Pro only shows early *church* devotion, Anti can still maintain post-Jesus development.
