# A-ANTI-010 — Humean miracle epistemology still dominates

**In short:** This argument supports [[C-A001|Resurrection did not occur]] via miracle epistemology. Even if some historical residues favor early Easter belief, Humean and related analyses keep bodily resurrection from rising to historical Proven—and can pressure overconfident Strengthened calls.

## Claim targeted

[[C-A001|Resurrection did not occur]] — Jesus did not rise from the dead as classical Christianity claims.


## Thesis

Even if some historical residues favor early Easter belief, Humean and SEP analyses of miracle testimony keep bodily resurrection from rising to historical Proven — and can Weakened overconfident Strengthened readings.

## Argument

[[S-023|Hume, Of Miracles]], [[S-102|SEP Hume on Religion]], and critical sections of [[S-101|SEP Miracles]] emphasize the special burden on miracle testimony: laws of nature as firm experience vs singular contrary reports. Combined with apparition models ([[A-ANTI-008|Apparitions and metaphysics undercut bodily claim]], [[S-027|Lüdemann, Resurrection of Jesus]]) and Gospel embossing ([[A-ANTI-006|Gospel distance allows legendary embossing]]), Anti holds [[C-A001|Resurrection did not occur]] remains competitive even after R02’s [[SE-004]] Strengthened [[C-006|Resurrection]]. Neutrals should not treat Strengthened as near-Proven.
