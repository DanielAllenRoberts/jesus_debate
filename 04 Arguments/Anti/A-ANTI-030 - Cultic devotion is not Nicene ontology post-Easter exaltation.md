# A-ANTI-030 — Cultic devotion is not Nicene ontology; post-Easter experiences drive exaltation

## Explain for the reader

Anti grants much of Hurtado’s *descriptive* case — early Jesus-devotion is real and historically striking — then drives a wedge: cultic practice ≠ Nicene ontology, and the best historical driver remains post-Easter exaltation experience rather than a Jesus who already taught Chalcedonian self-understanding. That keeps [[C-A003|Later Nicene construction]] Strengthened and blocks Proven on [[C-003|Son / atonement]].

## Claim targeted

[[C-A003|Later Nicene construction]] (pressure against Proven on [[C-003|Son / atonement]])

## Thesis

Even if dyadic devotion is early ([[S-143|Hurtado]], [[S-144|How on Earth]], [[S-141|Bauckham exchange]]), the pattern is compatible with exaltation-from-below models: followers, after believing God raised Jesus, include him in unprecedented honor. Later councils then construct ontological formulas the first generation did not yet possess.

## Argument

1. **Grant the early devotion facts.** Anti need not deny *marana tha*, Kyrios confession, or Shema-adapted duality. Hurtado’s strength is phenomenological history. Denying the data is a losing move; reframing what the data *entails* is the scoring move.

2. **Practice ≠ ontology.** Nicene homoousios answers Greek metaphysical questions about shared essence. First-century devotion answers liturgical and loyalty questions about who may be invoked and honored. Jumping from “they prayed involving Jesus” to “they already held Nicene ontology” confuses categories. Even Hurtado’s own popular summary ([[S-142|Themelios]]) notes centuries of doctrinal struggle *after* Paul to articulate the pattern — which Anti reads as evidence that packaging is later.

3. **Post-Easter driver.** The devotion cluster appears in circles already formed by resurrection proclamation. That fits exaltation christology: God vindicated/exalted Jesus; therefore honor him as Lord. Bauckham–Hurtado disagreement on *why* worship arises (roles vs. divine command to reverence the exalted one) itself shows the explanatory center is post-Easter interpretation, not a simple retrieval of Jesus’ private metaphysical self-claim ([[A-ANTI-019|Early devotion is post-Easter not Jesus self-claim]] line continued).

4. **Intermediary debate cuts both ways.** Bauckham argues intermediaries are weak analogies; Anti can reply that even if Jesus exceeds angels in ascribed role, *ascription after exaltation* remains a developmental achievement. Unprecedented ≠ underived from Easter experience.

5. **Score ask.** Confirm [[C-A003|Later Nicene construction]] Strengthened; refuse Proven on [[C-003|Son / atonement]]; no % move (**52.0%**).
